## 5. Falsifier attack

**Concrete failure modes**
1. **The adversarial matching is not rigorously well-defined, only finitely describable.** `H_no-trap` is "a flat patch whose future boundary is shaped so the marginal law of `V` is matched." No existence/uniqueness/measurability argument is given. Matching the `V`-marginal at every fixed `n` requires a boundary profile depending on `ρ_n`, the Poisson law, and the interior geometry. The memo asserts this is "always feasible" (audit §10.4) as a single DoF, but **matching the mean ≠ matching the marginal law**, and matching the marginal at each `n` does not imply a single limiting adversary. If matching is only approximate, the classification applies to an ε-perturbed adversary, and `UNDECIDED_NEEDS_<X>` may be premature because `(P_n,Q_n)` is not yet uniquely defined. [MEMO §3; audit §10.4]
2. **The four-case classification is for simple laws; the adversary is plausibly composite, and the deliberation never resolves the switch.** Memo §5 marks simple-vs-composite a "caveat"; the A7 charge lists it as an abstention condition. Emitting `UNDECIDED_NEEDS_<X>` without resolving it is a restatement of the same ambiguity in token form.
3. **EGS Eq. 14 (the only candidate separating statistic) is not a pure `σ(C_loc)` functional.** EGS itself used embedding info to separate ingoing/outgoing ladders (derived-md:594: "we deal with those configurations by making use of the information on the embedding").
