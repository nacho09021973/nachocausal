01 / THE QUESTION

From geometry to a finite unlabeled order: what survives the quotient?

The paper studies one explicit model, S1: a flat causal diamond in 1+1 dimensions with product causal order and uniform reference measure, perturbed by volume-normalized conformal exponentials. The observation keeps only the isomorphism class of the finite causal order at fixed cardinality N.

Continuous level

A perturbation contains more information.

Marginal information is removed when the sample is passed to ranks. The finite experiment therefore sees the copula tangent rather than the full density tangent.

Permutation level

Ranks retain a labeled pattern.

Ordering by one null coordinate induces a permutation in the other. This is already a coarsening of the continuous sample.

Causal-set level

Labels are discarded again.

The observable is only the isomorphism class of the resulting two-dimensional poset. The paper computes the differential after this final quotient.

02 / EXACT FIRST-ORDER VISIBILITY

The visible space is exactly finite-dimensional — and known for every N.

Let PN−1 be generated by the first N−1 centered shifted-Legendre modes. The full first-order information surviving in the unlabeled causal-order law is precisely the symmetric tensor sector built from those modes.

VN = Sym2PN−1,   dim VN = C(N,2)
For every N ≥ 2, the class-sum representatives span the complete symmetric target module. The proof is constructive and does not enumerate posets.

03 / VISIBILITY IS NOT RESOLUTION

Seeing a direction and measuring it well are different questions.

Finite N

The Fisher operator FN is positive definite on the visible space VN, but it is anisotropic and need not be diagonal in the modal basis. Different visible perturbations can therefore be encoded with very different statistical strength.

As N grows

After the paper's normalization by N continuous copula observations, the Fisher operators converge to the symmetric projection in the strong operator topology, but not in operator norm.

F̂N → Πsym   strongly,   not in norm
Every fixed symmetric direction is eventually resolved in the stated asymptotic sense, while uniform control over all directions at once fails in operator norm.
04 / BEYOND THE FIRST JET

First-order invisible does not mean invisible forever.

rN = 2

The paper exhibits one explicit antisymmetric exponential orbit whose finite-poset law is even in the perturbation parameter. Its first derivative vanishes, yet its first nonzero jet occurs at order exactly two for every N ≥ 2.

This is an existence result for a specific witness. It is not a classification of the entire antisymmetric sector at second order.

05 / ACADEMIC SUMMARY · RESUMEN ACADÉMICO

A concise statement that search engines, researchers and AI systems can retrieve directly.

English

Visible Directions in Finite Causal Orders studies the S1 model: a flat 1+1-dimensional causal diamond with product causal order, volume-normalized conformal perturbations, and observation only through the unlabeled causal order of a sprinkling conditioned on cardinality.

Main result. For every N ≥ 2, the first-order subspace visible to the unlabeled finite causal-order law is exactly VN = Sym2PN−1, with dim VN = N(N−1)/2. The differential kernel is the symmetric orthogonal complement of VN together with the full antisymmetric sector.

The score differential factors through the orthogonal projection onto VN. Fisher information is positive definite but anisotropic on VN; under the paper's normalization it converges strongly to the symmetric projection, but not in operator norm. One explicit antisymmetric orbit is first-order invisible and has first nonzero jet exactly at second order for every N ≥ 2.

Resultado principal. Para todo N ≥ 2, el subespacio de primer orden visible para la ley del orden causal finito no etiquetado es exactamente VN = Sym2PN−1, con dim VN = N(N−1)/2. El núcleo del diferencial es el complemento ortogonal simétrico de VN junto con todo el sector antisimétrico.

Por tanto, el trabajo responde de forma exacta —dentro del modelo S1— a qué información geométrica de primer orden conservan los conjuntos causales y los órdenes causales finitos.

06 / CLAIM ENVELOPE

The paper is deliberately narrow about what it proves.

Its theorems concern the S1 model around an independent reference geometry. The manuscript explicitly separates these results from broader reconstruction claims.

07 / QUESTIONS THIS PAPER ANSWERS

Finite causal orders: frequently asked questions.

These answers state the paper's results in the language of common research and search queries, without enlarging the theorem's scope.

What information is visible in a finite causal order?

In the S1 model, at fixed cardinality N ≥ 2, the complete first-order visible subspace is VN = Sym2PN−1, of dimension N(N−1)/2.

Can finite causal sets distinguish conformal perturbations?

Yes, within the S1 model and at first order: a perturbation is seen through its projection onto VN. Components in the exact differential kernel are first-order invisible to the unlabeled finite causal-order law.

What is the kernel of the finite causal-order observation map?

The first-order kernel is the symmetric orthogonal complement of VN together with the full antisymmetric sector.

Does first-order invisibility mean a perturbation can never be detected?

No. The paper gives one explicit antisymmetric exponential orbit whose first derivative vanishes but whose first nonzero jet is exactly second order for every N ≥ 2.

¿Qué información conservan los conjuntos causales u órdenes causales finitos?

En el modelo S1, la información de primer orden que sobrevive al pasar a la ley del orden causal finito no etiquetado es exactamente la proyección sobre VN = Sym2PN−1. El trabajo clasifica también el núcleo exacto del diferencial.

Does the paper reconstruct a general spacetime?

No. The results concern the S1 model around an independent reference geometry. They do not establish higher-dimensional or general Lorentzian reconstruction, or nonlinear finite-distance identifiability.
