# WP3 model families for causal-set identifiability

> **Documento de trabajo REVISABLE, no congelado.** Este documento define infraestructura

## 1. Scope and status

This WP3 note introduces candidate families of probability laws

`P_n(theta)`

over finite causal sets, equivalently finite posets interpreted as observed causal order. The
families are intended as clean objects for asking identifiability questions independently of the
current sealed estimator.

Status:

- `EXPLORATORY_MATHEMATICAL_INFRASTRUCTURE`;
- no empirical claim is made;
- no estimator result is used;
- no numerical fit quality is evidence here;
- no simulation is performed or requested by this document.

The object of study is a distribution `P_n(theta)` over finite causal sets. A sample from
`P_n(theta)` may be generated by a geometric or non-geometric mechanism, but the default observed
object is the finite order after labels and embedding data have been forgotten.

The immediate purpose is not to conclude that a horizon is recoverable. The purpose is to specify
families in which statements such as separation, contiguity, minimax lower bounds, or
single-instance non-identifiability can later be formulated without referring to a particular
pipeline.

## 2. Notation

Let `C_n` denote a finite causal set with `n` elements, represented as an isomorphism class of
finite posets unless stated otherwise. Let `theta in Theta` denote a parameter of a generative
family. A law

`P_n(theta) = Law(C_n | theta)`

is the probability distribution of the observed finite order under parameter `theta`.

When a geometric model is used, a latent construction may include:

- a continuum spacetime patch `M_theta`;
- a causal order `prec_theta` on `M_theta`;
- a sampling rule, typically but not necessarily a Poisson sprinkling or fixed-cardinality sample;
- latent coordinates, boundary labels, density data, or geometric fields.

Unless explicitly stated, the observation map forgets all labels, coordinates, embedding data, and
external geometric markers. It retains only the induced finite order.

Three information channels must be kept separate:

1. **Order-only data.** The observation is the unlabeled finite poset `C_n` up to isomorphism.
2. **Order + cardinality.** The observation includes the finite order and the value of `n`, or a
   distributional regime in which `n` carries volume information. This is weaker than full
   geometric input but stronger than treating cardinality as irrelevant.
3. **Order + external geometric input.** The observation includes the order plus information such
   as a known boundary, known surface, embedding coordinates, a selected antichain, or an
   externally supplied horizon. This channel is useful for benchmarking, but it is not the
   intrinsic order-only problem.

A geometric observable is a latent functional

`G(theta)`

such as presence of a horizon-like structure, location of a boundary, width of a near-horizon
band, singularity-truncation scale, or a coarse interior/exterior partition. A future
identifiability statement must name both the observation channel and the observable `G`.

For two parameters `theta_0, theta_1`, order-level indistinguishability questions are questions
about the pair of laws `P_n(theta_0)` and `P_n(theta_1)`, for example through total variation,
Hellinger distance, KL divergence, likelihood-ratio behavior, or contiguity.

## 3. Family A: positive geometric horizon family

### Definition A.1

Family A is a geometric family in which the latent spacetime patch contains a known
horizon-like structure before the embedding is forgotten.

A minimal version is:

- dimension fixed at `d = 2`, interpreted as `1+1D`;
- a parameter space `Theta_A` of finite Lorentzian patches `M_theta`;
- each `M_theta` contains a distinguished continuum structure `H_theta` that plays the role of a
  horizon-like null boundary or horizon-associated transition;
- finite samples are generated from `M_theta` by a specified sampling rule at size or density
  indexed by `n`;
- the observed object is the induced finite order after coordinates, labels, and the latent
  marker `H_theta` are forgotten.

Thus `P_n^A(theta)` is the law of the unlabeled finite poset induced by the sample from
`M_theta`.

### Parameter content

The parameter `theta` may include, depending on the subfamily:

- the position of the horizon-like structure inside the sampled patch;
- the size and shape of the patch;
- a density or fixed-cardinality convention;
- curvature or conformal data within the `1+1D` model;
- a singularity or terminal truncation rule, if present;
- boundary conditions that determine which parts of the latent spacetime are sampled.

These components should not be silently merged. In particular, horizon location, box boundary, and
singularity truncation are distinct latent features even when they produce correlated order
effects.

### Observation and forgetting

Default observation:

`C_n ~ P_n^A(theta)`

where `C_n` is the unlabeled finite order only.

Forgotten data:

- continuum coordinates;
- point labels and generation order;
- embedding map;
- the true location of `H_theta`;
- any external inside/outside annotation;
- any terminal boundary annotation.

Optional strengthened channels may be studied later, but they must be named as order+cardinality
or order+external-geometric-input variants.

### Identifiability question in Family A

For an observable `G(theta)` tied to `H_theta`, identifiability in Family A means that the laws
`P_n^A(theta)` contain enough information to infer `G(theta)` in a specified sense.

Examples of admissible formulations:

- **Detection:** distinguish `theta` with a horizon-like structure from an appropriate null model.
- **Coarse classification:** infer a coarse interior/exterior or near/far partition with error
  tending to zero under a named loss.
- **Localization:** estimate a latent horizon-associated position or band within scale `a_n`.
- **Testing separation:** show that `||P_n^A(theta_0) - P_n^A(theta_1)||_TV -> 1` for alternatives
  separated in `G`.
- **Lower-bound obstruction:** show that alternatives with different `G` remain close in TV,
  Hellinger, KL, or contiguity.

No such statement is asserted here. Family A only defines a positive geometric setting in which
such statements can be asked.

## 4. Family B: null/control non-horizon family

### Definition B.1

Family B is a control family with no true latent horizon. Its role is to model finite-order
features that can mimic, mask, or confound a horizon-associated signal.

A minimal version is:

- dimension fixed at `d = 2` unless a later document explicitly changes it;
- patches have causal order and finite boundaries but no distinguished horizon-like structure;
- samples are generated by the same cardinality or density convention used for a comparable
  Family A subfamily;
- the observed object is again the unlabeled finite order, with labels and embedding data
  forgotten.

The law is written `P_n^B(phi)`, where `phi in Theta_B` parametrizes non-horizon geometry or
non-geometric control structure.

### Confounding features

The parameter `phi` may include:

- finite box boundaries;
- spatial or temporal volume gradients;
- truncation effects unrelated to a horizon;
- terminal or singularity-like structure;
- inhomogeneous sampling intensity;
- corners, cutoffs, or asymmetric windows;
- non-geometric poset growth rules chosen to imitate selected order statistics.

These are not treated as nuisances to ignore. They are the point of the family. A horizon claim is
weak if the same order-level feature is also produced by a non-horizon control law.

### Role in identifiability

Family B enables questions of the form:

- Can order-only tests distinguish `P_n^A(theta)` from `P_n^B(phi)`?
- Which statistics respond to horizon-like structure rather than generic finite boundary effects?
- Does a proposed observable detect a horizon, or only a terminal future truncation?
- Are there non-horizon controls contiguous to positive horizon models?
- How much external geometric input is needed to separate the two families?

A future positive result should specify the control class. A future negative result should specify
whether indistinguishability is against all of Family B or only against a subfamily.

## 5. Family C: adversarial or near-non-identifiable family

### Definition C.1

Family C is a pair or continuum of models designed to make lower-bound and impossibility questions
explicit. It contains latent geometries or generative mechanisms with different geometric
observables but very similar finite-order laws.

A two-point version is:

`theta_0, theta_1 in Theta_C`

with

`G(theta_0) != G(theta_1)`

but with `P_n^C(theta_0)` and `P_n^C(theta_1)` close under a specified statistical distance.

A continuum version is a path `theta_t`, `t in I`, along which `G(theta_t)` changes while the
finite-order law varies slowly or remains asymptotically contiguous across small increments.

### Candidate constructions

Possible adversarial mechanisms include:

- shifting a horizon-like boundary by a sub-discreteness or `O(ell)` scale while preserving most
  induced order relations;
- changing the latent placement of a horizon and compensating with patch boundary geometry;
- replacing a horizon-associated future truncation by a non-horizon terminal truncation;
- pairing a geometric model with a non-geometric poset model matched on selected order
  statistics;
- constructing two completions of the same observed finite order with different latent geometric
  interpretations.

These are design patterns, not proved counterexamples.

### Role in lower-bound questions

Family C is the natural site for:

- two-point lower bounds;
- Le Cam arguments;
- Fano-type finite packing arguments;
- contiguity and likelihood-ratio calculations;
- claims that a scale `a_n` is not identifiable from order-only data under a named model class.

The family exists to prevent a false inference from estimator failure to intrinsic
non-identifiability. A lower bound must be stated against the laws `P_n^C(theta)`, not against the
current estimator output.

## 6. Questions enabled by WP3

These families make the following questions precise enough for later work:

1. **Single-instance identifiability vs ensemble identifiability.** Does a statement concern one
   observed finite causet, a sequence of laws, or averages over an ensemble?
2. **Detection vs localization.** Is the target merely the presence of a horizon-like structure,
   or the location of a boundary or band?
3. **Coarse classification vs fine localization.** Can an order-only rule classify broad regions
   while still failing to localize the latent boundary below scale `a_n`?
4. **Horizon signal vs singularity imprint.** Is the observed order feature tied to a horizon-like
   structure, to terminal future truncation, or to their coupling in a specific patch?
5. **Positive family vs null control.** Which features separate Family A from Family B, and under
   what channel of observation?
6. **Estimator limit vs family limit.** Does a failure belong to one rule, a class of rules, or
   the full law of the observed order?
7. **Dependence on dimension `d = 2`.** Which conclusions are artifacts of the `1+1D` setting,
   and which would even be meaningful in higher-dimensional families?
8. **Role of cardinality.** Does identifiability require order-only data, order plus `n`, or
   external volume information?
9. **Intrinsic finite-order lower bounds.** Are there pairs or continua of latent geometries whose
   finite-order distributions are too close to support arbitrarily fine inference of `G`?
10. **Boundary confounding.** Can box boundaries, truncation, or volume gradients induce the same
    order-level evidence as the proposed horizon observable?

## 7. Non-goals

This WP3 document does not assert:

- that the current estimator is optimal;
- that the current estimator is suboptimal;
- that a horizon is reconstructible from one causal set;
- that any horizon-like structure is identifiable from order-only data;
- that `R^2`, numerical fit quality, or regression stability is evidence for identifiability here;
- that the operational floor of any sealed estimator is an intrinsic lower bound;
- that Family A, B, or C has already been simulated;
- that a lower bound has already been proved;
- that `d = 2` results would automatically extend to higher dimension.

No simulations are part of this WP3 document. Any future simulation should be treated as a
separate empirical artifact with its own scope, controls, and status.

## 8. Minimal closure criterion for WP3

WP3 contributes a mathematical interface, not a result. It is adequate only if later work can
point to a named family `P_n(theta)`, a named observation channel, a named latent observable
`G(theta)`, and a named statistical criterion before making any claim about recoverability or
non-identifiability.
